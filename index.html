2. About Carbon Capture Technology

Carbon Capture Technology is a process used to capture carbon dioxide (CO2) before it enters the atmosphere or to remove it from the air.

Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. A high concentration of greenhouse gases contributes to global warming and climate change.

Why Is Carbon Capture Important?

  • It can reduce emissions from industrial activities.
  • It can support efforts to control global warming.
  • It can help industries move towards cleaner production.
  • It can support long-term climate goals.

Carbon capture is not a complete solution by itself. Reducing fossil fuel use, improving energy efficiency, and using renewable energy are also important.

3. How Does Carbon Capture Work?

Carbon capture systems separate carbon dioxide from gases produced by industries or power plants. The captured CO2 can then be transported and stored.

Main Steps

  1. Capture: Carbon dioxide is separated from industrial gases or directly from the air.
  2. Compression: The captured CO2 is compressed to make transportation easier.
  3. Transportation: CO2 is moved using pipelines or other suitable transport methods.
  4. Storage: CO2 is injected into suitable deep underground geological formations for long-term storage.

Simple Example

A factory produces gases containing CO2. A capture system separates much of this CO2, which can then be transported to a suitable storage location.

4. Types of Carbon Capture

There are different methods of capturing carbon dioxide. The suitable method depends on the source of emissions and the technology being used.

Type Description
Post-Combustion Capture CO2 is separated from gases after fuel is burned.
Pre-Combustion Capture Fuel is processed before combustion to produce a gas mixture from which CO2 can be separated.
Oxy-Fuel Combustion Fuel is burned in oxygen rather than ordinary air, producing exhaust that is easier to process for CO2.
Direct Air Capture Special systems remove carbon dioxide directly from the surrounding air.

Which Method Is Best?

There is no single best method for every situation. Cost, energy requirements, the amount of CO2 produced, and the location all affect the choice.

5. Applications of Carbon Capture

Carbon capture technology can be used in several industries where reducing CO2 emissions is difficult.

Major Applications

  • Power Plants: Capture CO2 from emissions produced during electricity generation.
  • Cement Industry: Reduce emissions from cement production.
  • Steel Industry: Help reduce emissions from steel manufacturing.
  • Chemical Industry: Capture CO2 from certain chemical production processes.
  • Direct Air Capture: Remove CO2 directly from the atmosphere.
  • CO2 Utilisation: Use captured CO2 in selected products and industrial processes.

Using captured CO2 in products does not always mean permanent carbon removal. The result depends on how the CO2 is used and whether it returns to the atmosphere.

6. Benefits and Challenges

Benefits

  • Can reduce carbon dioxide emissions.
  • Can help industries lower their carbon footprint.
  • May support climate change mitigation.
  • Can create opportunities for research and innovation.
  • Can complement renewable energy and energy efficiency.

Challenges

  • Building and operating capture systems can be expensive.
  • Some methods require a large amount of energy.
  • CO2 needs suitable transport and storage infrastructure.
  • Stored CO2 must be monitored for safety and leakage.
  • Not every industry or location is equally suitable.

Conclusion

Carbon capture has useful potential, especially for industries that are difficult to decarbonise. However, its success depends on cost, safe storage, good planning, and responsible use alongside other climate solutions.

7. Future Scope and Contact

Future Scope

Research is being carried out to make carbon capture more affordable, efficient, and reliable. New materials and improved capture systems may help reduce energy requirements and operating costs.

  • Development of better CO2 capture materials.
  • Improved monitoring of underground storage.
  • More efficient industrial capture systems.
  • Research into direct air capture technology.
  • Greater public awareness of climate solutions.
